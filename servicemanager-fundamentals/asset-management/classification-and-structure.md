# Classification and Structure

## Classifying and typing your assets

Every asset sits under one of Hornbill's asset classes - including Computer, Mobile Device, Software, Network Device, Printer, and Telecoms - which determines the attributes available to it. Within each class, administrators define asset types (for example, Laptop, Server, or a specific software product) and can create as many client-specific types as needed, each with its own name, prefix, and set of visible or mandatory attributes.

**Learn more:** [Asset Structure](https://docs.hornbill.com/servicemanager-user-guide/asset-management/asset-structure) | [Manage Asset Types](https://docs.hornbill.com/servicemanager-user-guide/asset-management/manage-asset-types)

## Tracking assets through their lifecycle

Assets move through defined states - Current, Active, and Archived - and administrators can add their own custom substates beneath each one (for example, "In Stock" under Current, or "Allocated" under Active) to reflect exactly where an asset sits in its lifecycle. This gives you both a consistent high-level status and the granularity to track your organization's own processes.

**Learn more:** [Manage Asset Types](https://docs.hornbill.com/servicemanager-user-guide/asset-management/manage-asset-types)

## The information you can capture on each asset

Out of the box, asset records capture location, owner, assigned ("used by") user, cost center, financial details such as cost, depreciation, and maintenance cost, manufacturer and model, serial number, and a client-defined asset tag - alongside class-specific detail such as warranty dates, license information, or network configuration. Administrators can add further client-specific attributes on top of this using the same asset type configuration.

**Learn more:** [Asset Structure](https://docs.hornbill.com/servicemanager-user-guide/asset-management/asset-structure)
