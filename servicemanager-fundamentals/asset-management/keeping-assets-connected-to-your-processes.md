# Keeping Assets Connected to Your Processes

## Tracking installs, moves, adds, and changes

Assets can be linked to incidents, requests, changes, and projects, with every change of ownership, location, or status captured automatically in the asset's history, giving you a running record of installs, moves, adds, and changes (IMAC) over the asset's life. Workflow automation can update linked asset attributes directly as part of processing these requests, rather than relying on someone remembering to update the asset separately.

**Learn more:** [Requests Automation](https://docs.hornbill.com/servicemanager-config/customize/workflows/requests-automation) | [Assets Action](https://docs.hornbill.com/servicemanager-user-guide/service-portfolio/requests/asset-action)

## Keeping status and location current

Where assets are imported from discovery and monitoring tools, their status and location can be validated and updated automatically each time the import runs, alongside the option for support staff to update either manually at any time.

**Learn more:** [Asset Data Import Tool](https://docs.hornbill.com/data-imports-guide/assets/overview)

## Identifying and managing asset discrepancies

The Configuration Item Explorer gives you a graphical view of an asset's relationships and dependencies, making it easier to spot assets that are missing expected links, out of policy, or otherwise inconsistent with what you'd expect. Combined with the "last discovered" data captured by the asset import process, this can be used to identify assets that haven't been seen for a defined period, and workflow automation can be used to trigger a defined process to investigate and resolve the discrepancy.

**Learn more:** [Configuration Item Explorer](https://docs.hornbill.com/servicemanager-user-guide/configuration-management/configuration-item-explorer)
