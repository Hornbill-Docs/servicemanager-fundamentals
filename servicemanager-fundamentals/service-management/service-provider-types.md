# Service Providers

While on the face of it, the job of a Service Desk is simple, customers can raise requests and the service provider fulfills the request - easy right?

Not so fast... in practice, there are a number of variations that appear subtle and not that important at first glance. Its probably a good idea to first define the fundamental goals of a Service Provider, the aspects that are truly common, which include:-

- __Clearly Defined Available Services__: It should be clear what services are available from the Service Provider for the Service Consumer to consume or receive.
- __Clearly Defined Provider/Consumer Relationship__: The relationship, service levels, and where appropriate, the service and/or contractual terms between the the Service Provider and Service Consumer mut be well defined, and understood by both parties. 
- __Relationship Management__: As well as providing the offered services, a Service Provider must typically manage the relationship between them, and each customer/service consumer they are providing services to. It is especially important to manage the relationship between the Service Provider and Service Consumer that is consuming and/or being provided with the services. The distinction between service provider types is very important when you get to the detail of managing the relationship between the service providers and their customers/service consumers/users. 
- __Records and Reporting__: Service Providers must keep high quality data in terms of communications history, service fulfillment and service level performance history, be able to report on all and any of that data as well as maintain audit trails for post-service analysis and compliance auditing, reporting and retrospective failure analysis. 

It is further worth noting that a very high-level categorization of Service Providers can be more broadly categorized as Operations Management and Customer Management. Broadly speaking, Operations Management is the category of systems, tools and workflows deployed to manage the operations of an organization.  Whereas Customer Management is the category of systems and workflows deployed to deliver and manage customer service/customer experience which is more generally associated with Customer Relationship Management (CRM) approach.  Of course like most things, there is a spectrum of variations here, but its always good to try and shape your services around well defined terms in order to ease communication and understanding of your service offerings. 

## Service Provider Types  (Operations Management)
Although each and every service provider is unique, they tend to fall into one of the following common categories.  Its also possible that one service provider can be operating in more than one of these models simultaneously, there are no hard set rules here, but when designing your services its important to try and create clarity around your operating mode, if you can describe it in writing in an unambiguous way, then you are on the right track. 

__Internal Service Desk__: A service desk is a centralized point of contact that provides support to users/Employees within an organization. It is a functional unit responsible for receiving, logging, and resolving or escalating various types of requests or issues related to the services that function provides or supports.  An organization is likely to have more than one Service Desk in operation, typically a Service Desk is functional and discrete, so IT, HR, Facilities and so on, each with their own tools, portals and service offerings as required. 

__Internal Shared Service Desk__: A Shared Service Desk (SSD) is a centralized point of contact or help desk that provides support services to users within an organization. It is a single contact point where employees (internal users) can report IT or other issues, request assistance, or seek information related to IT or other employee services. The purpose of an SSD is to consolidate and streamline the IT support function by centralizing the handling of service requests and incidents. An SSD aims to provide a consistent and efficient experience to users by offering a unified interface and standardized processes for IT and other employee support. An SSD typically handles a wide range of queries, including technical issues, password resets, software installations, and general inquiries.

__Shared Service Provider (SSP)__: A Shared Service Provider (SSP) is a service function provided by one organization that delivers common/shared services not only to its own organization but to other business units or strategically aligned external organizations who have similar service requirements. The goal of an SSP is to consolidate, streamline and reduce duplication of common functions or services that can easily be shared in order to achieve economies of scale, increase efficiency, and reduce costs. SSPs typically operate within a single organization and serve internal stakeholders. They may provide services such as IT infrastructure, human resources, finance and accounting, procurement, and other back-office functions. The services provided by an SSP are shared among multiple business units or strategically aligned organizations, allowing the group of organizations to collectively benefit from centralized expertise, shared resources, and often significant cost savings. 

__Managed Service Provider (MSP)__: A Managed Service Provider (MSP) is an external company or organization that delivers ongoing management and/or support for specific services or systems. MSPs are typically engaged by an organization that wish to outsource certain aspects of their operations to a specialized service provider. MSPs are commercial organization who's business model is built around selling common/shared services and business process outsourcing arrangements on a commercial basis to other companies. MSPs take on the responsibility of managing, resourcing and delivering a wide range of services on behalf of their clients, essentially acting as an extension to their clients own workforce.  MSPs may offer a wide range of IT and other business services, but are often specialists in on or more areas like IT, HR, Payroll and others. Customers outsource these services to MSP's because an MSP, through centralized specialist knowledge, expertise and economies of scale, are able to deliver the same or better service at a lover overall cost than the customer organization is able to achieve.   MSPs offer their services primarily on a commercial basis, and operate under contractual service-level agreements (SLAs) that define the scope, quality, and performance metrics of the services they provide in exchange of commercial consideration. The commercial nature of the relationship between an MSP and its Customers often require comprehensive CRM-like capabilities for managing communications, documentation, contractual issues and performance reporting. 

:::note
It is very easy to put SSP's and MSP's into the same bucket, because at a high-level, they appear to share a lot of the same characteristics. However, in reality they are quite different operations that need fundamentally different approach in day to day management of the services they provide.  Stepping away from product functionality for a moment, The fundamental difference between a Shared Service Provider (SSP) and a Managed Service Provider (MSP) is the strategic intent, governance and commercial objectives of the service provider.  An MSP typically offers a commercial service, to one or more external organizations, providing agreed set of services on a commercial/contractual basis.  An MSP monetizes its business by maintaining specialist knowledge and expertise as well as economies of scale that makes it possible to offer the same service as a customer can do in-house at a lower cost, or a higher-quality service at similar cost.

Shared Service Providers (SSPs) on the other hand are typically formed out of a strategic partnership, typically between similar organizations with the goal of centralizing and optimizing common services, reducing costs, unifying technology stacks, business processes and other sharable service elements that achieve higher operational efficiencies and therefore reducing overall costs to all members of the strategic alliance.
:::

## Service Provider Types  (Customer Management)
Service Providers in this category focus on providing services to external customers. The tools and workflows in this category deliver on the experience your customers receive from you as part of the product/services you provide to them.

__Customer Service Desk__: A customer service desk is generally geared towards providing services to external customers in support of a product or service being provided to that customer.  The classic model here would be a product or software company that sells its products/services to a business or consumer who provides technical/user support for that product.  Customer Service Desks, while related to Service Desks generally really fall outside of the Operations Management Arena and are better defined in the Customer Relationship Management (CRM) category.

__Citizen Services__: A citizen service desk is typically deployed by public bodies to provide the general population with access to public services. A typical public body will provide a wide range of services, some delivered by in-house, some delivered by external service providers and where possible delivered by automated means. What characterizes Citizen Services is the nature of the relationship between the Service Provider and the Citizen.  Most often, individuals on first contact, or even each contact are not known to the service provider.  Instead each individual service offered has different point of content verification/authorization requirements that vary from none at all right through to requiring face to face or telephone verification.  Unlike most other service desk type deployments, Citizen services typically present a single, simplified and unified interface, often built from a content management capability, to their citizens, where the incoming requests and inquiries are then routed/distributed to the correct functional area and/or systems for fulfillment.
